Mark 10:32–52

Bible Study Guide  ·  Search Ministry

"Whoever wants to be great among you must be your servant.
Whoever wants to be first must be slave of all." — Mark 10:43–44

The Passage at a Glance

Three scenes. One thread: What does it actually look like to follow Jesus?

VersesScene
32–34Jesus predicts his death — and walks toward Jerusalem anyway
35–45James and John ask for the best seats. Jesus reframes greatness.
46–52Blind Bartimaeus cries out, gets silenced, cries louder — and is healed.

Scene 1: Walking Toward the Hard Thing (v. 32–34)

The disciples were astonished and afraid. Jesus was out ahead of them. He knew exactly what waited in Jerusalem — he described it in full detail — and he went first anyway.

Discussion Questions

  • What's the thing you know you need to do, but you're walking behind on rather than in front of?
  • Jesus didn't pretend it would be easy. How does honesty about cost change the way you make commitments?
  • Who in your life is watching how you walk toward hard things?

Scene 2: The Ambition Trap (v. 35–45)

James and John weren't villains. They were ambitious men who genuinely believed in Jesus — and still asked for the best seats. That's very human. Jesus doesn't shame them. He redirects them.

"Whoever wants to be great among you must be your servant.
Whoever wants to be first must be slave of all." — v. 43–44

The world ranks men by power held. Jesus ranks them by power given away.

Discussion Questions

  • Where in your life are you most tempted by status — work, family, church, community?
  • The other disciples got angry at James and John. But were they angry because it was wrong — or because they wanted the seats too?
  • Name someone in your life who leads through service. What does that actually look like day to day?
  • What would change this week if you measured success by how you served rather than how you were seen?

On the "Cup" and "Baptism" (v. 38–39)

Jesus asks if they can drink the cup he'll drink. They say yes without knowing what they're agreeing to.

  • Have you ever said yes to something — marriage, fatherhood, faith — and only later understood the cost? What got you through?

Scene 3: The Man Who Wouldn't Be Quiet (v. 46–52)

Bartimaeus is blind, poor, sitting on the side of the road. He hears Jesus is passing. He starts shouting. The crowd tells him to shut up.

He shouts louder.

Jesus stops. Asks what he wants. Bartimaeus doesn't ask for money or food — he asks to see. Jesus heals him. Bartimaeus immediately follows him down the road.

Discussion Questions

  • What are you crying out for that you've gotten quiet about — because life got loud, or people told you to dial it down?
  • The crowd silencing Bartimaeus was the same crowd following Jesus. People close to faith can still shut down someone else's cry. Have you been on either side of that?
  • He asked to see — not for security or comfort, but for clarity. What would it mean to ask God for clarity on something specific right now?
  • He followed immediately after being healed. No pause, no plan. What would you do differently if you actually believed the answer was yes?

The Thread Across All Three

Jesus is heading toward the cross. His disciples are jockeying for status. A blind man on the roadside sees more clearly than any of them.

What are you blind to?
And are you willing to shout for clarity — even when it's uncomfortable?

Closing — Personal Takeaway

Complete one of these honestly:

1.  "The hard thing I need to walk toward this week is..."

2.  "One place I'm leading through status instead of service is..."

3.  "The thing I need to stop being quiet about and bring to God is..."
